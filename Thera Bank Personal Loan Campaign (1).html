The model is 90.1% accurate when ran with a liblinear solver which is a good accuracy. The precision is very high as well with a value of 97.6%. However the false positive rate is .64 and the auc is .64 so the original model is not a good model to determine how many customers would take a personal loan. These are adequate metrics to analyze the model as banks stand to gain from accurately finding people likely to take out the loan, but do not lose much from type 2 errors. Though, with a newton-cg model the auc score increases to .76 and the accuracy increases to .94. Thus the model has improved.Changing the tolerance or the random_state count does not affect the model. The lbfgs regression model decreases the accuracy marginally to .92, the false positive rate to .55, and the auc_score to .71. This is comprable but I would still prefer a higher auc score and a higher true positive rate in this case as there would be a better chance to identify people who would be likely to take a loan.

Business Perspective¶

From a business viewpoint the current model is the best model as the most important error trpe is the type 2 error. This would mean that the company woiuld be losing out on potential business from people who would have actually taken the loan. The current model has the highest auc score for the roc curve which means that the true positive rate will stay high while the false positive rate will be minimized. Thus, the company can accurately advertize the loan campaign to the correct type of customer. This would save the company time and money in the long run.
